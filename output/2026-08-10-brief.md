## Frontier / Foundation Models (21)

### California

**SB 1106** -- Agentic artificial intelligence.  
This bill adds "agentic AI" — systems that can take actions on their own rather than just generate content — to two existing state AI oversight processes: the Office of Emergency Services' risk analysis of AI threats to critical infrastructure must now cover it in its recommendations, and the Department of Technology's annual inventory of high-risk automated systems must now list agentic AI that state agencies use, are building, or are buying. Practically, it means agencies have to track and report a new category of AI tools they may not currently be cataloging.

**SB 813** -- California Artificial Intelligence Standards and Safety Commission: artificial intelligence safety standards.  
The bill creates a new state commission (by July 2027, housed in the Business and Consumer Services Agency) to write the rules governing companies that audit AI systems — including what audit standards they must meet, when their registration can be suspended or revoked, and what qualifies one as an independent verifier. It only takes effect if AB 1405 passes, and if AB 1709 passes instead, the new commission isn't created and its duties fall to that bill's e-Safety Advisory Commission.  
*(Summary based on the official digest/abstract.)*  
Latest action: August 5 set for first hearing. Placed on suspense file. · [source](http://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/billNavClient.xhtml?bill_id=202520260SB813)

**AB 412** -- Generative artificial intelligence: training data: copyrighted materials.  
This bill would require companies that build generative AI models to set up a web portal where copyright holders can ask whether their specific works were used as training data, and to answer within 30 days with a list of the holder's works that were used. Developers who miss the deadline rack up a separate violation for each additional day and can be sued by the rights holder (after a chance to fix the problem), with exemptions for models trained only on data the developer makes freely public.  
*(Summary based on the official digest/abstract.)*  
Latest action: In committee: Referred to APPR. suspense file. · [source](http://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/billNavClient.xhtml?bill_id=202520260AB412)

**AB 2653** -- State contracts: report: modern foundation models and associated artificial intelligence systems.  
This bill sets up a nine-member state working group, led by the Department of Industrial Relations with the Department of Technology, to examine the working conditions behind the development of AI foundation models — including the low-paid data labeling and content moderation work often done overseas — and to recommend whether California should apply labor standards to its purchases of AI systems, similar to its existing anti-sweatshop rules for apparel. The group must deliver a report to the Legislature by December 31, 2027; it does not itself impose any new requirements on AI vendors or change state procurement rules.

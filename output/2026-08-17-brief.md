**AB 412** -- Generative artificial intelligence: training data: copyrighted materials.  
This bill would require companies that build generative AI models to set up a website portal where copyright holders can ask whether their specific works were used as training data, and to answer within 30 days with a list of any of that owner's materials used. Missing the deadline counts as a separate violation for each day it continues, and copyright owners could sue for damages after giving the developer notice and a chance to fix it, though models trained only on free, publicly available data the developer itself posts are exempt.

This bill limits AI in therapy to administrative and support roles: it requires patient consent before AI can record, transcribe, or screen people for therapy needs; bars AI from making therapeutic decisions or assessing a patient's emotional state without a licensed clinician's review and sign-off; and prohibits marketing chatbot-delivered services as psychotherapy. It also bars companies from selling, sharing, or training AI models on therapy session data in violation of medical privacy law, with enforcement handled by the relevant licensing boards.

**AB 2575** -- Health care services: artificial intelligence.  
Starting July 1, 2027, hospitals, clinics, and physician practices using AI-based clinical decision support tools would have to give doctors, nurses, and other staff who work with those tools — on request — a list of all such systems in use plus details on how each one generates its recommendations, and notify those workers of this right at hire and annually.
